# Credits, upstream code and provenance

FreeRig builds on substantial work by open-source audio developers. **We do not claim the imported algorithms, circuit research or recorded cabinet responses as our own.** Our work includes the Windows host, rig/scene integration, native adapters, presentation, presets, routing, validation and the modifications described below. Some FreeRig processors are compositions of several upstream engines.

This inventory describes the source used here, not a claim that entire upstream applications are bundled. Research candidates such as BYOD, FunBox, LSP and SwankyAmp are not included merely because a research guide mentions them. Names of commercial equipment describe source targets or visual families; they do not imply endorsement, proprietary source access or verified exact emulation.

## FreeRig adaptations and compositions

FreeRig adds host wrappers, C ABI integration, saved patch/scene contracts, routing, parameter/preset descriptions and interface controls around imported processors. Shimmer Hall combines Signalsmith shifting with Dragonfly Hall. Other FreeRig adapters include EQ, manual/envelope wah, reverse/granular processing, vocoder and practice looping. These additions do not make the underlying third-party engines original FreeRig research. Source and porting notes show the actual implementation and any arithmetic changes; offline comparisons do not prove commercial hardware equivalence.

## Artwork, AI assistance and external services

The illustrated hardware PNGs were generated with an AI image tool, using equipment-family references. They are not photographs or exact bespoke chassis for every processor. [Hardware design](HARDWARE-DESIGN.md) records scope, references and [prompts](hardware-artwork-prompts.json). The application's controls are live interface elements layered over the artwork. Screenshots in this repository show the app, rather than promising exact real-world equipment reproduction.

AI coding assistants have contributed to implementation, documentation and testing. Human instructions, assistant handovers and validation notes remain visible in the repository. Automated or offline checks are described as such; they are not presented as live ASIO listening, measured round-trip latency or an independent quality endorsement.

TONE3000 is an optional external service, not our capture catalogue or an upstream DSP engine. Its branding belongs to its owner. Downloaded captures retain their creators' credit and separate permissions. Manufacturer and pedal names are used descriptively; proprietary algorithms such as Strymon TimeLine/BigSky are not included or claimed.
